Aim:

AVL Tree ADT.

Theory:

An AVL Tree is a self-balancing binary search tree where the difference between the heights of left and right subtrees of any node (called the balance factor) is at most 1. Named after its inventors Adelson-Velsky and Landis, AVL trees ensure faster operations compared to unbalanced binary search trees by maintaining this balance.
Rotations in AVL Tree
To maintain balance, the tree undergoes rotations when an insertion or deletion disrupts its balance. There are four types of rotations:
  1. Left Rotation (LL Rotation):

    2. When nodes are inserted into the right subtree, causing a right-heavy imbalance.

  2. Right Rotation (RR Rotation):

    3. When nodes are inserted into the left subtree, causing a left-heavy imbalance.

  3. Left-Right Rotation (LR Rotation):

    4. When a node is inserted into the left subtree of the right child, requiring a left rotation followed by a right rotation.

  4. Right-Left Rotation (RL Rotation):

    5. When a node is inserted into the right subtree of the left child, requiring a right rotation followed by a left rotation.

Algorithm:

  1. Insertion in AVL Tree
    1. Perform a standard BST insertion.
    2. Update the height of the current node.
    3. Calculate the balance factor:
      • If balance factor > 1(left-heavy tree)
        • Perform Right Rotation (RR) if the left child is left-heavy.
        • Perform Left-Right Rotation (LR) if the left child is right-heavy.
      • If balance factor < -1 (right-heavy tree):
        • Perform Left Rotation (LL) if the right child is right-heavy.
        • Perform Right-Left Rotation (RL) if the right child is left-heavy.
  2. Deletion in AVL Tree
    1. Perform a standard BST deletion.
    2. Update the height of the affected node.
    3. Calculate the balance factor and apply necessary rotations similar to insertion.
