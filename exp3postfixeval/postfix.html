Aim:

Evaluate postfix expressions using a stack.

Theory:

Postfix evaluation is a method of evaluating expressions where operators come after their operands. In a postfix expression like "ab+", the operands 'a' and 'b' are followed by the operator '+'. To evaluate a postfix expression, we use a stack data structure.

Algorithm:

  1. Initialize an empty stack.
  2. Scan the postfix expression from left to right.
  3. If the scanned character is an operand, push it onto the stack.
  4. If the scanned character is an operator, do the following:
    1. Pop two operands from the stack. These are the two most recent operands that were pushed onto the stack.
    2. Perform the operation using the two operands popped from the stack:
      • The first operand popped will be the right operand.
      • The second operand popped will be the left operand.
      • Apply the operator to the two operands (e.g., `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`).
    3. Push the result of the operation back onto the stack.
  5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 until all characters in the postfix expression are processed.
  6. At the end of the expression, there will be exactly one value left in the stack, which is the result of evaluating the postfix expression.
  7. Print the value remaining in the stack. This will be the result of the postfix evaluation.
